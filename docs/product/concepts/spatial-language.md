# Spatial Language

**Layer:** meaning added by the author, made close to the body.

## Hypothesis

People can reason together more effectively when abstract relationships become
objects, paths, volumes, and transformations that can be jointly inspected and
manipulated. In a hologram these belong to the meaning layer: each primitive is
anchored to something in the event (a wrist, a prop, a place, a moment) and
keeps its meaning from every viewpoint.

## Communication Primitives

- Force arrows show direction and intensity.
- Constraint rings show permissible movement or range.
- Risk clouds show uncertainty, hazard, or unresolved conditions.
- Dependency bridges show relationships between ideas or components.
- Timeline ribbons show sequence and causality through time.
- Compare mirrors put alternatives in the same shared frame.
- Question beacons identify unresolved points.
- Decision seals preserve a chosen path and its rationale.

Each primitive also defines how it appears at every step down: in a headset at
true scale, through a phone portal, on a flat screen, and in a still image.

## Product Directions

- Start with a small built-in symbol vocabulary before enabling fully custom symbols.
- Prefer primitives made during the performance: a gesture becomes an editable
  primitive bound to what it was drawn around, rather than a menu insertion
  afterwards.
- Let a creator spawn, bind, transform, and link primitives while speaking.
- Treat symbols as interactive objects that can reveal text, evidence, media, or
  a deeper simulation. Content without a place (a chart, a document) joins by
  being attached to a primitive or referent.

## Open Questions

- Which primitives are universal across training, design review, and social use?
- Does a shared symbol vocabulary emerge naturally or require explicit teaching?
- How can the scene remain readable when several people create artifacts at once?
- Which primitives still read on a flat phone without moving the portal?
