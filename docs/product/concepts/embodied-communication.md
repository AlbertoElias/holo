# Embodied Communication

**Layers:** event (capture), meaning read from the body.

## Hypothesis

Presence becomes useful when the recipient can read and act on cues that are
lost in text or flat video: body orientation, timing, gaze, gesture, scale,
voice prosody, and relation to nearby objects or people. Because a hologram
records the body as data rather than pixels, those cues are not only visible
from any angle; they can be made explicit (where a finger points, how big a
gesture was) without the author adding anything.

## Product Directions

- Capture face, gaze, hands, body, and voice as one coordinated event, at true
  scale. Avatar size must match the performer, or pointing rays and
  measurements become wrong.
- Read meaning from the body before asking for it: pointing and gaze resolve to
  referents, demonstrated motions leave paths, sizing gestures become
  measurements.
- Preserve human scale and spatial audio in VR, AR, and portal playback on
  flat screens.
- Let creators amplify meaning with expression markers where the body's own
  delivery was not enough.
- Mark what was recorded, what was inferred (synthesized locomotion, filled
  tracking gaps, read-from-body links), and what was edited.

## Design Tension

Higher realism can improve social presence, but it can also trigger discomfort,
privacy concerns, and uncertainty about authenticity. Fidelity must be chosen
for communication benefit, not as an end in itself. The same applies to
inference: every cue the system reads from the body can be wrong, so it must
be visible as inferred and easy to dismiss.

## Open Questions

- Which cues most improve comprehension for remote instruction?
- Which body signals (pointing, gaze, sizing, pace) can be read reliably enough
  to show without confirmation?
- What is the acceptable boundary between expressive editing and deceptive editing?
- How should consent, recording scope, and replay rights work?
