# HoloIM Product Vision

## Thesis

**A hologram is a recording of an event, not of a view.** A video stores what
one camera saw. A hologram stores what happened: a person's body, voice, and
attention, in a place, over time, at true scale. Every view of it is made when
someone watches, so a video can be moved through in time, and a hologram in
time **and in space**.

That makes holograms interactive, embodied argument spaces: people communicate
through presence, spatial relationships, gesture, objects, and shared
attention, and the audience can look from wherever they choose. The same
communication can happen live in VR, live in AR, or asynchronously, and on a
flat screen the hologram is a portal into its world rather than a clip.

The promise is not that every message should become volumetric. Text, images,
and video remain better for many tasks. Holograms win when the message depends
on what a fixed view would lose:

- Human scale, distance, orientation, or physical context.
- Gesture, posture, gaze, or emotional delivery.
- Interaction with objects, paths, or environments.
- A need to inspect from another angle, question, or manipulate the explanation.
- A need to establish shared reference rather than merely broadcast information.

The full model, the three layers (event, meaning, view), and the test every
idea must pass are in [concepts/what-a-hologram-is.md](concepts/what-a-hologram-is.md).

## Product Principles

1. **The body is the native language.** Movement, eye line, voice, and hands
   carry meaning. Capture them faithfully at true scale, and make explicit what
   the body already said before asking the author to add anything.
2. **Anchor to the event, never to the screen.** A note, symbol, or reply is
   tied to a person, object, place, or moment, so it keeps its meaning from
   every viewpoint and on every device.
3. **The author directs attention, never the camera.** Authors say what matters
   and where it is; each device decides how to show it. Viewers choose Follow
   (the device carries out the author's intent) or Free (they move through time
   and space themselves).
4. **The audience participates in the same medium.** Viewers inspect from any
   angle, ask, and reply; a reply is a new event placed at a moment and place
   inside the original.
5. **Live and async are one system.** A live session is an event being
   recorded; afterwards it is replayable, editable, and repliable without
   losing context.
6. **Everything has a place.** Text, images, charts, video, objects, audio, and
   simulation join a hologram by being given a place and a moment: held, set
   on a surface, or pinned to a referent.
7. **Recorded, inferred, and added stay distinguishable.** Trust depends on
   knowing what the author did, what the system read from their body, and what
   was added afterwards.
8. **Clarity outranks spectacle.** Presence and visual invention are useful only
   if they reduce ambiguity, improve recall, shorten handoffs, or enable better
   decisions.

## Communication Model

A holographic utterance is more than audio and motion. It has intent, a
referent in the event, an action in space, a time scope, a confidence level,
an expected response, and a provenance (recorded, inferred, or added). This
model, **HoloGrammar** in the idea registry, is the schema of the meaning layer.

## What Success Looks Like

- A recipient understands a spatial or embodied explanation with fewer
  clarification cycles than from a video and supporting documents.
- A phone viewer moves around a hologram, not just plays it, and finds the
  referent the author meant without asking.
- A team can return to a past conversation, see what was referenced, and
  understand the decision path.
- A creator can improve a message without re-recording an entire performance.
- A remote participant feels present enough to coordinate attention, intent,
  and action with people in a real or virtual place.
