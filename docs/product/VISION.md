# HoloIM Product Vision

## Thesis

Holograms are not 3D videos. They are interactive, embodied argument spaces:
people can communicate through presence, spatial relationships, gesture,
objects, simulations, and shared attention. The same communication can happen
live in VR, live in AR, or asynchronously as a replayable hologram.

The promise is not that every message should become volumetric. Text, images,
and video remain better for many tasks. Holograms win when the message depends
on one or more of these properties:

- Human scale, distance, orientation, or physical context.
- Gesture, posture, gaze, or emotional delivery.
- Interaction with objects, paths, or environments.
- A need to inspect, question, or manipulate the explanation.
- A need to establish shared reference rather than merely broadcast information.

## Product Principles

1. **Embodiment is semantic.** Body movement, eye line, voice, and hand motion
   carry meaning and should be captured, preserved, and editable.
2. **Space is a shared reference system.** A comment attached to an object or
   moment should retain its meaning when the viewer changes perspective.
3. **The audience participates.** Viewers should be able to inspect, reply,
   reconstruct their understanding, and make decisions in the same medium.
4. **Live and async are one system.** A live spatial conversation should become
   an editable, searchable, replyable asynchronous artifact without losing
   context.
5. **Media is composable.** Text, images, charts, video, 3D objects, audio, and
   simulation should work as layers of one holographic message.
6. **Clarity outranks spectacle.** Presence and visual invention are useful only
   if they reduce ambiguity, improve recall, shorten handoffs, or enable better
   decisions.

## Communication Model

A holographic utterance is more than audio and motion. It has intent, a
referent, an action in space, a time scope, a confidence level, and an expected
response. This model is called **HoloGrammar** in the idea registry.

## What Success Looks Like

- A recipient understands a spatial or embodied explanation with fewer
  clarification cycles than from a video and supporting documents.
- A team can return to a past conversation, see what was referenced, and
  understand the decision path.
- A creator can improve a message without re-recording an entire performance.
- A remote participant feels present enough to coordinate attention, intent,
  and action with people in a real or virtual place.
