# Moodboard references

Supporting research for IDEA-031 ([design language](../concepts/design-language.md)). The live board, with images, reactions and your own additions, is the [HoloIM Moodboard](https://claude.ai/artifact/6ofHzWfmFmQTiQXBryNfMU) (private until shared). This file is the plain list, grouped by the design question each reference helps answer, so it can be re-created elsewhere (for example Are.na, which does not allow automated access). Images on the board come from Wikimedia Commons under the licences noted there; games and films are linked, not copied.

## Holograms and light

How a person made of light can feel warm and present rather than sci-fi.

- [Blade Runner 2049: Joi](https://www.awn.com/vfxworld/double-negative-delivers-joi-blade-runner-2049): They rejected the blue scanline hologram. Joi is light that falls off to black, built from the back shell of her body.
- [Pepper's ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepper%27s_ghost): The 19th-century stage trick behind most "holograms": a reflection that reads as a presence in the room. Image: Bertrand (no first name given) · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [The Weather Project, Olafur Eliasson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Project): An indoor sun. Warm, shared light as a material: the solarpunk warmth a hologram could carry.
- [James Turrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Turrell): Light treated as a physical substance you stand inside.
- [teamLab](https://www.teamlab.art/): Digital light that responds to people, with flowers and water that bloom around you.
- [Looking Glass](https://lookingglassfactory.com/): Light-field displays: a hologram is parallax, each viewer sees their own view.
- [SuperSplat](https://superspl.at/): Gaussian splats: real places captured as soft clouds of light. The likely look of an AR world slice.
- [Bioluminescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioluminescence): Living light: soft, organic glow instead of neon. Image: art farmer from evansville indiana, usa · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- **Ours: Greenhouse hologram in a living room**: Round 3 render: golden light rising from a mossy brass stage.

## Containers

What a message arrives as, and where it lives when it is not playing.

- [Wardian case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardian_case): The Victorian sealed glass case that carried living plants across oceans. A message as a living thing in glass. Image: Unknown · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Terrarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrarium): A whole small ecosystem you can hold and look into. Image: Unknown · CC BY-SA 2.5 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Snow globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_globe): A tiny world you hold, shake and watch settle. Image: Michael Nutt · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Joseph Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cornell): Shadow boxes: small, poetic worlds behind glass.
- [Ship in a bottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship-in-a-bottle): Patience and craft sealed inside glass; a gift to marvel at. Image: Robert A. Hoff (ParkPennies.com) · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Message in a bottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Message_in_a_bottle): The original asynchronous message: sent into the world, found later. Image: w:National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Fabergé egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faberg%C3%A9_egg): A crafted container with a surprise inside: opening it is part of the message. Image: Guy Fawkes · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Pop-up book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-up_book): A message that unfolds into a scene when opened. Image: Hans Hartung · CC BY-SA 2.0 de · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Diorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diorama): A frozen scene to look into; how a hologram could rest when it is not playing. Image: Unknown authorUnknown author · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- **Ours: terrarium arriving on a table**: Round 3 render: a received message as a small glass terrarium with the sender inside.

## Summoning tools

Menus and palettes that bloom, unfold or live on the hands.

- [Open Brush (Tilt Brush)](https://openbrush.app/): The wrist palette: one hand holds the tools, the other paints.
- [Half-Life: Alyx design details](https://roadtovr.com/these-details-make-half-life-alyx-unlike-any-other-vr-game-inside-xr-design/): How small physical details make your hands feel real.
- [Meta: hands UI best practices](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/): Palm-up pinch opens the options menu; poke what is near, point at what is far.
- [Ultraleap XR design guidelines](https://docs.ultraleap.com/xr-guidelines/): Hand menus of three or four items, feedback before contact, very few poses.
- [Apple WWDC23: Design for spatial input](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10073/): Eyes and hands as input, and when direct touch beats looking.
- [Apple WWDC23: Principles of spatial design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10072/): Depth, scale and immersion basics, worth knowing so we can depart from them on purpose.
- [Gravity Sketch](https://www.gravitysketch.com/): Two-handed creation in VR and how tools stay close to the hands.
- [ShapesXR](https://www.shapesxr.com/): Fast spatial prototyping; how it surfaces tools without big panels.
- [Pie menu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pie_menu): Marking menus: directional choices that experts flick without looking.
- **Ours: palm bloom**: Round 3 render: actions bloom as petals from an upturned palm.

## Marks and symbols

Arrows, paths, glyphs and symbols that live in space.

- [Arrival: the heptapod logograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptapod_languages): Circular symbols carrying a whole idea at once. A model for marks that live in space, not in lines of text.
- [How Arrival's language was made](https://gizmodo.com/how-arrival-turned-linguistics-into-one-of-the-most-gri-1789009881): Sketches, linguists and about 100 logograms: how a symbol system gets designed.
- [Isotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(picture_language)): Otto Neurath's picture language: a small vocabulary anyone can read. Image: Otto Neurath · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Labanotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labanotation): A way of writing down movement. Marks for gesture and time. Image: AnonymousUnknown author · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Ōkami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckami): The celestial brush: draw a symbol and the world responds.
- [Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game)): Gestures drawn in the air cast miracles.
- [Tiny Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade): Rough strokes become graceful walls: the tool embellishes for you.
- **Ours: a vine drawn while talking**: Round 3 render: a route drawn across the stream becomes a leafy vine.

## Time

Ways to see, hold and move through time.

- [The Horse in Motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Horse_in_Motion): Muybridge: motion sliced into readable moments. Image: The Library of Congress · No restrictions · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Étienne-Jules Marey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne-Jules_Marey): Chronophotography: a whole movement in one image. Image: Félix Nadar (1820-­1910) · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Zoetrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope): A ring of moments that comes alive when spun. A time ring around the stage. Image: Unknown · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Prague astronomical clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_astronomical_clock): Time as a beautiful mechanism of rings and figures. Image: Steve Collis from Melbourne, Australia · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Dendrochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrochronology): Tree rings: growth as a record of time. Image: Arpingstone · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Flip book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip_book): Time you hold in your hand and riffle through. Image: John Barnes Linnett · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Braid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_(video_game)): Time you can rewind and play with.
- **Ours: vine ring around the stage**: Round 3 render: reviewing a take with time as a vine ring, buds for moments.

## Presence and companions

Characters as interface, warmth, and being noticed.

- [Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_(video_game)): The imp: a squishy cursor with a face you draw yourself.
- [Kodama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodama_(spirit)): Tree spirits from Japanese folklore, the little forest presences in Princess Mononoke. Image: Toriyama Sekien · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Howl's Moving Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howl%27s_Moving_Castle_(film)): Calcifer: a warm, grumpy fire who lives with you and runs the house.
- [Sky: Children of the Light](https://thatgamecompany.com/sky/): Warmth without words: emotes, and candles given as gifts.
- [Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game)): Anonymous companionship through chirps and presence alone.
- [Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)): A warm, human-feeling future interface, designed with Geoff McFetridge.
- [Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_(video_game)): Quill acknowledges the player: a character who notices you.
- **Ours: the companion**: Round 3 render: a leaf-winged companion offering tools over your palm.

## Materials and palette

Solarpunk and other warm-future materials, colour and pattern.

- [Solarpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solarpunk): The movement: optimistic, green, crafted futures. Image: Dameon Hudson · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [The aesthetics of solarpunk worlds](https://solarpunkworld.substack.com/p/the-aesthetics-of-solarpunk-worlds): Art Nouveau, Ghibli and daylight, with a warning about vines on everything.
- [Alphonse Mucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mucha): Art Nouveau posters: flowing curves, halos, botanical frames. Image: Alphonse Mucha · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Tiffany lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_lamp): Stained glass light: warm colour that glows from within. Image: Unknown · CC0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Kunstformen der Natur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstformen_der_Natur): Ernst Haeckel's plates: nature's own geometry and symmetry. Image: Original: Ernst Haeckel. Scan: Ragesoss. Cleanup: Ilmari Karonen. · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Strawberry Thief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry_Thief): William Morris: dense, joyful botanical pattern. Image: The original uploader was VAwebteam at English Wikipedia. · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Palm House, Kew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_House,_Kew_Gardens): The Victorian glasshouse: iron, glass and living green. The Greenhouse direction in one building. Image: Diliff · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Gardens by the Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardens_by_the_Bay): Supertrees: technology and plants grown together. Image: Shiny Things. · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Bosco Verticale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosco_Verticale): A vertical forest: buildings that grow.
- [Stained glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stained_glass): Coloured light shaped by craft. Image: PtrQs · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Castle in the Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_in_the_Sky): Ghibli: overgrown technology in warm daylight.
- [Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausica%C3%A4_of_the_Valley_of_the_Wind_(film)): Ghibli: nature and technology in uneasy balance, with luminous forests.

## Type

Lettering that feels human and legible in a headset.

- [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/): Real-world type pairings. Search "Art Nouveau", "botanical", "handwritten".
- [Optima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optima): Hermann Zapf's humanist sans: classical, warm and legible. Image: GearedBull at en.wikipedia · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Atkinson Hyperlegible](https://www.brailleinstitute.org/freefont/): Designed for legibility at low resolution, which is where headsets are.
- [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau): Period lettering and ornament: whiplash curves and organic forms. Image: Iste Praetor · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons.

## 2D compression

Little worlds on flat screens: how a hologram survives on a phone.

- [Monument Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game)): Little architectural worlds on a phone screen.
- [Townscaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townscaper): A tiny town you build by tapping; a world small enough to hold.
- [Captain Toad: Treasure Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Toad:_Treasure_Tracker): Rotate a diorama level to see around it.
- [Kirigami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirigami): Paper scenes that rise out of a flat sheet: depth from 2D.
- **Ours: the hologram on a phone**: Round 3 render: the phone looks into the hologram as a small world on its stage.

## Motion and feedback

How things appear, respond to touch and settle.

- [Twelve basic principles of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation): Squash, stretch, anticipation and follow-through: how things feel alive.
- [Miura fold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_fold): Unfolds in a single motion: how a container could open. Image: Dmcq · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons.
- [Mimosa pudica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimosa_pudica): A plant that folds when touched: feedback before and at contact. Image: Suyash.dwivedi · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons.

## Anti-references

What we do not want to become.

- [The classic sci-fi hologram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holography_in_fiction): Blue, flickering, scanlined. What a hologram looks like when nobody thought about it.
- [Minority Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Report_(film)): Arms-up gesture walls: theatrical and tiring.
- [visionOS glass panels](https://www.apple.com/visionos/): Great platform UI, but copying it would make us generic.
- [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/): A vertical video feed: what a hologram must not become.
